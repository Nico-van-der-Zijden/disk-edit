# G64 — GCR-encoded 1541 image

A G64 stores a 1541 disk at the raw GCR (Group Coded Recording) level rather than as decoded sectors. This preserves custom GCR encoding used by copy protection schemes that don't fit the standard 256-byte-sector model.

## When to use G64 over D64

- The disk has **copy protection** — non-standard track length, custom sync, half-tracks, scrambled sectors. D64 can't represent these; G64 can.
- You want **byte-equal round-trip with an emulator** — VICE mounts G64s with full GCR fidelity.

A regular game / utility disk is fine as D64 and most users prefer that — smaller file, faster operations, easier to edit.

## What the editor does with G64

**Open**: Decodes the GCR back to sectors so you can browse the directory, view files, etc. The raw GCR bytes are kept in memory alongside the decoded view.

**Edit**: Sector-level edits are re-encoded to GCR on save. Sectors that the editor couldn't decode on open (e.g., custom GCR for copy protection) keep their original raw bytes — the protection survives the round-trip.

**Save**: Produces a real GCR-encoded G64 that VICE will mount as a 1541 image. Earlier versions of the editor wrote D64 bytes under a .g64 extension — that was wrong; saved G64s now have the proper format.

## Viewers

- **Disk → Disk Tools → G64 Layout…** opens a viewer that shows physical sector order per track, with a plain-English summary like "Mastered disk with copy protection: 4 tracks hide sectors behind custom GCR". Tracks are colour-coded standard / interleave-1 / copy-protected / scrambled.
- The **Raw Tracks** tab in the G64 Layout viewer renders the disk as concentric tracks coloured by underlying GCR bits — sync marks red, normal data green, gap/padding blue. Zoom 1× → 5× with click-and-drag panning.

## Saving

**File → Save** defaults to `.g64` when the tab was opened from a G64. If you opened a `.d64` and want a `.g64` instead, use **Save As**.

## Known limitations

- **Half-tracks** are rare in the wild and the editor's G64 reader/writer handles full tracks reliably. Half-track support is approximated; if you have a half-track copy-protected disk, the protection may or may not survive.
- **Per-byte speed offsets** are also rare; the editor renders them at the average track speed. V-MAX and Vorpal tracks specifically may not round-trip perfectly — we have no sample to validate against.
- The G64 format is well-documented; for serious copy-protected disk work, **nibtools** + a real 1541 + a parallel cable remains the gold standard.
